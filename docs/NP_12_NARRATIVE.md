# NP-12 — NARRATIVE ENGINE: The Story Bible

**Project Northpaw design vault · Doc 12/13**
Related: [[NP_01_SPORT]] · [[NP_07_HOUSES]] · [[NP_08_STABLE]] · [[NP_09_TRANSFORMATIONS]] · [[NP_13_SCOPE]]

Half the answer to "why do I love this" is the story machine: underdog stakes at minute one, every rival a themed philosophy, escalation with zero shame, and a collection system that IS the story system. This doc: premise, cast, the three-arc beat structure (Arc 1 fully beaten out), the rival club parade, the WTF-arc design space, themes, and tone law.

---

## 1. TONE LAW (binding)

1. Played dead straight, always (Doctrine D1). The narrative never winks at its own absurdity; characters treat a twelve-year-old's arena-freezing haymaker as the most important thing in the world, because it is.
2. Sincerity over irony; sentiment earned through setup, spent without shame.
3. Canadian texture is lived-in, not postcard: rink politics, billet families, February, Tim's runs as character beats, the specific ache of talent in a small town.
4. Comedy: character-based, warm, frequent — the Inazuma register (deranged training methods, dramatic rivals who are also kind of dorks).
5. Real cultures are written with the [[NP_07_HOUSES]] §9 standard; real pain (funding cuts, class, belonging) is honored, never solved by a montage.

## 2. PREMISE

**Harbourline** ([[NP_02_TWO_LAYER]] §1) is a hockey town. Hockey has the arena, the money, the front page. Rotation boxing — the sport that fifty years ago put this town on television — has the **Millbrook Boxing Club**: a condemned building, a coach who won't talk about why he quit, and exactly one member. The CRBF's letter is on the door in scene one: *demonstrate a competitive club by season's end, or the charter — the town's oldest — is dissolved.* The elite academy two towns over would be happy to absorb the charter's history. Survival dual, minute one. Endou's setup with a Canadian ache: it's not that nobody loves the sport — it's that the town *used to*, and stopped.

## 4. ARC 1 — THE CHARTER SEASON (fully beaten)

**Shape:** club survival → Regionals → Provincials → Nationals. Systems unlock in step (tiers per [[NP_09_TRANSFORMATIONS]]).

1. **Ep1 — The Letter.** Cold open: the 1976 televised dual that made the town (playable flashback tutorial — you play the *legend*, full-powered, then smash-cut to the present's empty gym: loss aversion as hook). The letter. First scrap. Priya recruited.
2. **The Survival Dual** vs. Ascendant's B-squad — a mini-dual you are *supposed to lose on paper*; winning any 2 segments satisfies the charter clause (the game's first taste of "segments have separate stories").
3. **Building five** — recruitment episodes (Dex; the community-centre arc; first house content). Each recruit episode is also a system tutorial (bonds, boards, tactics) — mechanics taught as friendship, the Inazuma pedagogy.
4. **Regionals pool** — Grain Kings (Yuri), the Netmen, a decoy-seal coach (tactics lesson). First remix rematch.
5. **Mid-season wound** — the gym fails inspection *anyway*; funding quest; the town starts showing up (crowd-heat system narratively activated: the meter on the screen is literally the town coming back).
6. **Provincials** — Montréal Technique semifinal (Marta; the feud detonates: why Whitlock quit, part 1). **Zone unlock** here: taught by failure, earned in the rematch.
7. **Nationals** — True North quarterfinal (Sam; the Iron dual format), Ascendant final. **You lose the final** — close, legitimate, on a Vey counter-pick masterclass. The charter survives (finalist standing), the rivalry is founded, and Arc 1 ends on the promise, not the trophy. (Doctrinal: the first arc of every great shonen sports story ends short of the summit.)

## 5. ARC 2 — THE HOLLOW CIRCUIT (the WTF arc — design space, beats loose by intent)

The series-signature escalation. An invitational circuit appears — the **Hollow Circuit** — run by a masked syndicate, staged in Blind-dual format ([[NP_01_SPORT]] §8), fielding clubs that should not exist: fighters with *erased* houses, techniques from dead lineages, and an anchor whose Wall Gauge doesn't reform — it *migrates* to teammates. Threads: the CRBF's sealed-records vault; House Iron's extinction; Whitlock's 1998 fixed dual and the friend the record says died; the protagonist's sealed house — all one story. The Commissioner's project: proving lineage can be *extracted* — technique without family, inheritance without love (the thematic inversion of your whole game). Set-pieces: Spirit Manifestation debuts per character; the legacy Inheritance episode (Whitlock's departed friend — the mechanic in [[NP_09_TRANSFORMATIONS]] §5 exists for this scene); the sealed-house reveal as the arc finale. Aliens remain a legal escalation option for Arc 2's back half if the room wants them; the syndicate structure is built to survive either answer. Inazuma earned aliens by episode 27 — the door stays open.

## 6. ARC 3 — TRUE NORTH (worlds)

Team Canada assembled from every rival fought: the parade of Arc 1–2 bosses in one locker room, feud graph and all (the roster IS the payoff of the collection system — [[NP_08_STABLE]]'s endgame is this room). International clubs as new pattern-boss philosophies (design room: world boxing traditions as visiting *houses*, written by the same [[NP_07_HOUSES]] §9 standard). The Vey thesis fight resolves here — Ascendant's synthesized machine vs. your forged bonds — either as the qualification gate or, strongest version, with Vey's academy *absorbed into* Team Canada and the thesis argued from inside the same corner. Awakenings ([[NP_09_TRANSFORMATIONS]] §6) ladder through the bracket; the protagonist's house resolves fully; the final dual returns to the ep-1 flashback's imagery — the town's television moment, fifty years later, yours.

## 8. DELIVERY STRUCTURE

Episode-shaped chapters (cold open → overworld act → dual act → tag scene), 24 in Arc 1. Cutscene budget tiers: nationals-level duals get full presentation; side duals run in-engine with broadcast framing. Bark/scene coverage requirements per named character are content-DB tracked ([[NP_13_SCOPE]] §5). Localization note from day one: FR-CA is a *first-class* script, not a port — Montréal Technique's scenes are written bilingually as characterization.

## 9. THEMES (the room's north stars)

Inheritance vs. invention (what do we owe the people who taught us) · The town that stopped believing · Synthesis with love vs. synthesis without it · No-names and bloodlines · And under all of it, the Inazuma engine: *it matters because your friends are watching.*
